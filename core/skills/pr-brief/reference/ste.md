# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (summary)

Use these rules for every sentence above the appendix when `language` in
the `PR Brief` section of `jig.config.md` is `ste`. They summarize the
writing rules of ASD-STE100. They do not replace its dictionary of approved
words. They add to the brief format's rules for every language, which still
apply.

## Words

- Use simple, common words, each with one meaning and one part of speech.
  Use the same word for the same thing every time ("defect" in one sentence
  means "defect" in the next; do not switch to "bug" or "issue").
- Technical verbs of the trade ("merge", "deploy", "commit") are allowed as
  written.
- Do not use contractions, idioms, slang, metaphors or phrasal verbs that have
  a one-word form ("find out" → "find").
- Do not use an "-ing" word as a noun ("the merging of" → "when you merge").
- Use no more than three nouns in a row. Break a longer noun cluster with
  "of", "for" or a relative clause.
- Use articles ("the", "a") and demonstratives where English needs them. Do
  not leave out words to make a sentence shorter.

## Verbs

- Use the active voice. Use the passive only when the actor is unknown or not
  important, and never in an instruction.
- Use the simple tenses: present, past and future. Use the present tense when
  possible.
- Do not use "-ing" verb forms to make tenses ("is checking" → "checks").
- Use the past participle only as an adjective ("the merged PR") or with
  "is", "are" or "was" in descriptive text.

## Sentences

- Write one topic per sentence.
- An instruction (a recommended action, a step, a required change) has 20
  words or fewer and gives one instruction, unless two actions happen at the
  same time. Use the imperative.
- A descriptive sentence has 25 words or fewer.
- Put a condition before the instruction it controls ("If X, do Y").
- Put no more than 6 sentences in one paragraph. Start each paragraph with
  its topic sentence.

## Structure

- Use a vertical list for a sequence of steps or a set of items. Number steps
  in the order a person does them.
- Write a warning or caution as a short command first, then the reason.
